# Safety and security automations

These recipes add information and supplemental actions. They do not replace listed alarms, approved safety controls, professional monitoring, practiced emergency plans, or a person checking the situation.

## Start with alerts

1. Detect and name the exact location.
2. Alert the responsible people immediately.
3. Keep the incident visible until someone acknowledges it.
4. Treat unavailable or conflicting data as unknown.
5. Add automatic control only when the equipment is approved for that job and recovery has been tested.

## Safety rules

- Never delay a smoke, carbon-monoxide, leak, or other urgent alert while waiting for confirmation.
- Never infer identity, permission, occupancy, or safety from one sensor.
- Keep normal locks, switches, appliance controls, and emergency equipment available.
- Do not bypass listings, interlocks, warnings, photo-eyes, or manufacturer instructions.
- Report a command as successful only after the final state is confirmed.
- Require a person to clear an incident when a dry, closed, cool, or quiet sensor cannot prove the problem is over.

## Before enabling control

- Test alert-only behavior first.
- Test unavailable, stale, low-battery, power-loss, hub-restart, and internet-loss states.
- Document the manual response and recovery path.
- Have the right qualified professional review electrical, fuel, plumbing, alarm, or door equipment where required.
- Let the household practice without creating a real hazard.
