## Good first lighting automations

Start with one room and one job. These are easy wins because you can see right away whether they work:

1. [Lights on with motion](/automation/lighting/lights-on-motion.html) in a hallway, bathroom, or closet.
2. [Lights off after motion stops](/automation/lighting/lights-off-after-motion.html) in the same room, with a forgiving timeout.
3. [Outdoor lights at sunset](/automation/lighting/outdoor-night-lights.html), so nobody has to remember the porch switch.
4. [Wake-up lights](/automation/lighting/wake-up-lights.html) that brighten slowly before your set wake time.

## FAQ

### What is a good first lighting automation?

Lights on with motion in a hallway, bathroom, or closet. You see right away whether it works, and the wall switch still works if it does not.

### Do I need smart bulbs or smart switches?

Either can work. Smart switches keep the wall control familiar for everyone, while smart bulbs suit lamps and color. The [bulbs vs switches article](/articles/smart-bulbs-vs-smart-switches-power-outages.html) covers what each does after a power outage.

### Why do my motion lights turn off while I am still in the room?

Usually the timeout is too short or the sensor cannot see someone sitting still. Lengthen the timeout, re-aim the sensor, or add a presence sensor. See [why motion lights turn off](/articles/why-motion-lights-turn-off.html).

### Will the wall switch still work?

It should. Every recipe here keeps the familiar wall control working and lets a manual change win over the automation.
