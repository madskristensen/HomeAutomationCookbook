# Sunrise alarm with smart bulbs

{% include content-hero.html src="/assets/img/social/guides/sunrise-alarm-smart-bulbs.svg" alt="A bedside lamp brightening slowly before a morning alarm time" %}

Yes, you can schedule smart lights to wake you. Start a bedroom light very dim about 30 minutes before your wake time, then raise it slowly to full brightness. You need a bulb or dimmer that dims low and fades smoothly. The [gradual wake-up lighting recipe](/automation/lighting/wake-up-lights.html) has the full rule.

## The short answer

| Choice | Good starting point |
|---|---|
| Light | A bedside lamp or ceiling light you can dim smoothly |
| Start | About 30 minutes before you want to be up |
| Length | 15 to 30 minutes. Longer for deep sleepers |
| Color | Warm at first, then cooler, if the bulb supports it |
| Skip | A simple toggle for days off |

## Pick a light that can do it

- **Dims low.** Some bulbs cannot start dim enough. Test the lowest level at night.
- **Fades smoothly.** Some setups jump in steps. Check that your platform and device support a gradual transition.
- **Color temperature (optional).** A bulb that goes from warm to cool looks more like a sunrise.
- **Smart bulb or smart dimmer.** Either works. A dimmer keeps the wall switch normal. A bulb can change color. See [smart bulbs vs. smart switches](/articles/smart-bulbs-vs-smart-switches-power-outages.html) for the tradeoffs.

## Set the timing

Subtract the fade length from your wake time. For a 6:30 AM wake time and a 30 minute fade, start at 6:00 AM. Use a day-of-week condition if weekends differ.

## Keep it kind to the household

- Use one lamp facing away from a partner who sleeps later.
- Cap the top brightness below full.
- Make sure the wall switch or app can always turn it off.

## If your platform has a built-in wake light

Some lighting apps and voice assistants offer a wake-up or sunrise option. If yours does, it may be the quickest start. Check it on your own bulbs before you rely on it, since features differ by app and device.

## Failure modes

- **Too bright at the start:** Lower the starting level, or turn the lamp away from the bed.
- **Jumps in steps:** Use a transition command, or try a different bulb or dimmer.
- **Runs on a day off:** Check the day condition and the skip toggle.

## FAQ

### Can I schedule smart lights to wake me up?

Yes. Schedule the light to start dim before your wake time and brighten over 15 to 30 minutes.

### Do I need a special sunrise alarm clock?

No. A smart bulb or dimmer with a scheduled fade does the same job, and you can use lights you already own.

### Does a sunrise light help light sleepers?

It replaces a sudden alarm with slow light. Keep it to one lamp and lower brightness if others in the room should stay asleep.
